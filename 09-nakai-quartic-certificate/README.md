## Ordinary homogeneous addendum

[Read the four-page addendum](homogeneous-addendum/paper/homogeneous-operator-addendum.pdf) · [Addendum sources and replay](homogeneous-addendum/README.md) · [Independent verification](homogeneous-addendum/verification/README.md)

For every ordinary homogeneous polynomial `f` of degree at least two in at least three variables over an arbitrary characteristic-zero field `k`, with the actual gradient quotient `k[x]/(partial_i f)` finite-dimensional, the displayed operator on `k[x]/(f)` has order at most two and lies outside the full algebra generated by multiplication and every quotient derivation. Word length is unrestricted. The theorem is `NakaiHomogeneousFiniteExpected.expected_finite_gradient_full_operator_separation`.

Hessian nonmembership is proved inside Lean by homogeneous variable syzygies, adjugate row identities, determinant comparison and pure powers extracted from a finite homogeneous quotient. It is not assumed as a theorem premise, and no classical socle theorem is imported as an unproved dependency. All sixteen addendum modules and the explicit statement fixture passed an independent fresh-output rebuild and a further replay of the portable publication script. Only the three standard Lean axioms occur in the final audits. The exact formal hypothesis is `Module.Finite` of the gradient quotient; no separate geometric equivalence theorem is claimed.

The operator's prior attribution is unchanged. This addendum expands the verified ordinary-homogeneous scope; it does not formalize the weighted extension or resolve the general Nakai conjecture. The original paper and all fifteen concrete proof sources below are preserved byte-for-byte.

## Original mixed-quartic result

For the actual quotient ring

`A = Q[x0,x1,x2,x3] / (sum_i xi^4 + sum_{i<j} xi^2*xj^2)`,

the certificate constructs the explicitly specified Hessian-adjugate operator on A, proves that every triple commutator with multiplication vanishes, and proves that the operator is outside the full Q-algebra generated by multiplication and **every** Q-derivation of A. Arbitrary finite words and their linear combinations are included.

The final declarations are `NakaiQuartic.quartic_full_operator_separation` and `NakaiExpected.expected_full_operator_separation`. The second independently spells out the literal ten-term quartic, actual quotient, multiplication maps, commutators, polynomial operator and unrestricted generating set. Neither statement assumes the desired separation conclusion.

The proof descends the operator on every polynomial representative, proves Jacobian-ideal stability under all actual quotient derivations, and separates the operator using a finite rational coefficient functional. The functional annihilates every polynomial multiple of each gradient generator and evaluates to -77760 on the Hessian determinant. No finite-sample replacement for arbitrary multipliers is used.

## Scope and prior work

The original six-page paper supplies a written ordinary-homogeneous theorem using the classical complete-intersection Jacobian-socle result. Its historical formal-scope limitation is superseded by the separate addendum above, which proves the required nonmembership implication internally under the exact finite-gradient-quotient hypothesis. The original fixed-quartic proof continues to use its explicit coefficient certificate.

The three-variable operator formula already appears in Diethorn et al., arXiv:2209.13110v3, Sections 4.1.3 and 4.5, equations (33), (35), and Theorem 4.5.1, up to the explained normalization. In every dimension, the ordinary-homogeneous specialization of Yau–Zhu–Zuo, arXiv:2604.24508v4, gives the same operator, up to the factor 1/2 explained in the review. Jacobian-ideal descent is explicit in Li–Xiao–Zuo, arXiv:2502.04672v1, Theorem 2.7; their Remark 1.1 also records the filtered/full-word distinction. Stable-ideal methods are prior art. No publication priority, previously unknown singularity class, or resolution of the unrestricted Nakai conjecture is claimed.

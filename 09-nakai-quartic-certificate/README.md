# A formal Jacobian ideal certificate for a mixed quartic

**Completed fixed-ring theorem, verified in Lean. The unrestricted Nakai conjecture remains open in this work, and historical novelty is not claimed.**

[Read the paper](paper/nakai-quartic-certificate.pdf) · [LaTeX source](paper/nakai-quartic-certificate.tex) · [Reproduce the proof](lean/README.md) · [Verification record](verification/README.md) · [Prior-art review](reviews/prior-art-review.md)

## Exact completed result

For the actual quotient ring

`A = Q[x0,x1,x2,x3] / (sum_i xi^4 + sum_{i<j} xi^2*xj^2)`,

the certificate constructs the explicitly specified Hessian-adjugate operator on A, proves that every triple commutator with multiplication vanishes, and proves that the operator is outside the full Q-algebra generated by multiplication and **every** Q-derivation of A. Arbitrary finite words and their linear combinations are included.

The final declarations are `NakaiQuartic.quartic_full_operator_separation` and `NakaiExpected.expected_full_operator_separation`. The second independently spells out the literal ten-term quartic, actual quotient, multiplication maps, commutators, polynomial operator and unrestricted generating set. Neither statement assumes the desired separation conclusion.

The proof descends the operator on every polynomial representative, proves Jacobian-ideal stability under all actual quotient derivations, and separates the operator using a finite rational coefficient functional. The functional annihilates every polynomial multiple of each gradient generator and evaluates to -77760 on the Hessian determinant. No finite-sample replacement for arbitrary multipliers is used.

## Scope and prior work

The paper also supplies a written theorem for isolated ordinary homogeneous hypersurfaces in at least three variables, with its classical complete-intersection Jacobian-socle import stated explicitly. That full class is not formalized here. The fixed quartic uses an explicit coefficient certificate instead.

The three-variable operator formula already appears in Diethorn et al., arXiv:2209.13110v3, Sections 4.1.3 and 4.5, equations (33), (35), and Theorem 4.5.1, up to the explained normalization. Stable-ideal methods and related Hessian constructions are prior art. No publication priority, previously unknown singularity class, or resolution of the unrestricted Nakai conjecture is claimed.

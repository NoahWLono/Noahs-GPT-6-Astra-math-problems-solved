\begin{abstract}
The affine-power exclusion in the accompanying prime-inner-exponent paper
immediately extends its irreducibility conclusion to every inner exponent
whose prime divisors exceed the outer prime. Repeated prime factors are
allowed. This addendum records the short root-field argument, the exact Lean
endpoint, and the verification boundary of the new source. No originality
claim is made, and the full fixed-gap Pillai conjecture remains unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and scope}
For a nonzero integer $k$ and a rational prime $\ell$, let $v_\ell(k)$
denote the exponent of $\ell$ in $|k|$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:main}
Let $p\ge5$ be prime, and let $m>1$ be a natural number such that
\[
 q\mid m,\quad q\text{ prime}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q>p.
\]
Let $k,A\in\Z$ satisfy
\[
 |k|>1,\qquad v_\ell(k)<p\quad\text{for every prime }\ell\mid k.
\]
Then $(X^m-A)^p+k$ is irreducible over $\Q$.
\end{corollary}

Both $k$ and $A$ may have either sign, and $A=0$ is permitted.
There is no assumption $\gcd(m,k)=1$ and no squarefree condition on $m$.
In particular, primes dividing $m$ or the outer prime $p$ may divide $k$,
subject only to the displayed valuation bounds. For example, with $p=5$
and $m=49$, the polynomial $(X^{49}-A)^5+7$ is irreducible for every
integer $A$.

The extension is an immediate corollary of the previously established
arithmetic exclusion and a standard binomial criterion. It does not add a
new coefficient-integrality hypothesis or an assumed affine-exclusion
callback to the final statement. The inherited arithmetic argument is in
\emph{Irreducibility of a family of shifted binomial compositions}; the
original sealed source bundle is identified in Section~\ref{sec:record}.

\section{Proof in the actual root field}
\begin{proof}
Since $2\le p$, the condition on prime divisors implies $2\nmid m$.
Thus $m$ is odd. The prime $p\ge5$ is also odd.

The valuation hypotheses imply that $k$ is not a $p$th power in $\Q$:
any prime divisor $\ell$ of $k$ has $0<v_\ell(k)<p$, whereas a rational
$p$th power has valuation divisible by $p$. The prime-binomial criterion
therefore makes $T^p-k$ irreducible over $\Q$. As $p$ is odd, changing the
sign of the variable also proves that
\[
                         f(T)=T^p+k
\]
is irreducible.

Let $\alpha$ be a root of $f$, and work in its actual simple root field
$K=\Q(\alpha)$. Put $\theta=-\alpha$. Then $\theta^p=k$,
$K=\Q(\theta)$ and $[K:\Q]=p$. Consequently every $\beta\in K$ admits a
reduced representation
\[
             \beta=G(\theta),\qquad G\in\Q[T],\qquad \deg G<p.
\]
For each prime $q\mid m$ we have $q>p$. Applying the inherited checked
affine-power exclusion to this representation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:no-power}
                \beta^q\ne A-\theta=A+\alpha
                \qquad\text{for every }\beta\in K.
\end{equation}
This is the same signed arithmetic endpoint used by the original paper;
its hypotheses are precisely the prime and valuation assumptions above.

The standard odd-binomial irreducibility criterion says that, for odd
$m$, the conditions \eqref{eq:no-power} for every prime divisor $q$ of $m$
imply the irreducibility of
\[
                       X^m-(A+\alpha)\quad\text{over }K.
\]
It applies to arbitrary odd $m$, including those with repeated prime
factors. With $g(X)=X^m-A$, this binomial is exactly $g(X)-\alpha$.
The monic composition criterion now gives the irreducibility over $\Q$ of
\[
                     f(g(X))=(X^m-A)^p+k,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

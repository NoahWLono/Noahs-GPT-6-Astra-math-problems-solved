# Prior-art review: order-ten binary 3-frameproof codes

Review cutoff: **10 October 2026**.

Guo, Stinson and van Trung explicitly left the order-ten case open in 2015. The present result proves that every binary `SHF(10;10,2,{1,3})` is a permutation matrix up to independent row complementation. The literature reviewed through the cutoff above did not reveal an earlier resolution of this exact classification. **Full-text coverage is incomplete, particularly for Sun–Wang (2025), so this review does not establish priority or guarantee novelty.** Proof correctness and prior-art status are separate questions.

The question concerns a binary matrix with ten rows and ten columns. For every four pairwise distinct columns $a,b,c,d$, there must be a row $r$ satisfying

$$
A_{ra}\ne A_{rb},\qquad A_{ra}\ne A_{rc},\qquad A_{ra}\ne A_{rd}.
$$

Rows represent coordinates and columns represent codewords. Equivalence permits row permutations, column permutations, and independent row complementation. A trivial example is a permutation matrix; these equivalences give all trivial examples. The question asks whether any other example exists. It imposes no design, constant-weight, linearity, or automorphism assumption. A bound allowing at most ten codewords, without classifying the equality case, would not settle it.

The historical source is Chuan Guo, Douglas R. Stinson and Tran van Trung, *On Symmetric Designs and Binary 3-Frameproof Codes*, in *Algebraic Design Theory and Hadamard Matrices*, Springer Proceedings in Mathematics & Statistics **133** (2015), pp. 125–136, DOI [10.1007/978-3-319-17729-8_10](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-17729-8_10). Concluding problem 3, on published page 136 and page 12 of the [author manuscript](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~dstinson/papers/sdesign-5.pdf), states: “The case v = 10 remains open.” The same discussion identifies the uniqueness results through order nine and a nontrivial order-eleven example from a symmetric $(11,5,2)$ design. This is an explicitly posed case in the literature.

The following are the most directly relevant checked bounds. Here $N$ or $n$ denotes code length, and $w$ or $t$ denotes coalition size, following each source's notation.

| Source and inspected result | Scope relevant to this question | Substitution for coalition size three |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guo–Stinson–van Trung, *On tight bounds for binary frameproof codes* (2015) | For $w\ge3$ and $w+1\le N\le3w$, the maximum number of codewords is $N$, with equality only for a permutation matrix in standard form. | Gives the classification for $4\le N\le9$. |
| Shangguan–Wang–Ge–Miao, *New Bounds for Frameproof Codes* (2017), Theorem IV.1 | For $w\ge2$ and binary $w$-frameproof codes with $N<\binom{w+1}{2}$, the number of codewords is at most $N$; equality has the permutation-matrix characterization. | Requires $N<6$. |
| Zhao–Zhang, *Improved Upper Bounds for Wide-Sense Frameproof Codes* (2024), Theorem 8 | Restates the earlier binary uniqueness result for $t\ge3$ and $3\le n\le3t$. | Reaches $n=9$. |
| Zhao–Zhang (2024), Theorem 11 | For $t\ge3$ and $3\le n<\frac{15+\sqrt{33}}{24}(t-1)^2$, a wide-sense code of size $n$ has the permutation-matrix characterization. | The upper threshold is $\frac{15+\sqrt{33}}6<3.458$. |

These statements were checked in the public manuscripts: [Guo–Stinson–van Trung](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.6920), [Shangguan–Wang–Ge–Miao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5782), and [Zhao–Zhang, version 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05596v1). Their publication details are, respectively, *Designs, Codes and Cryptography* **77** (2015), 301–319, DOI [10.1007/s10623-015-0037-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-015-0037-y); *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* **63** (2017), 7247–7252, DOI [10.1109/TIT.2017.2745619](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2017.2745619); and *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* **70** (2024), 8636–8646, DOI [10.1109/TIT.2024.3411150](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2024.3411150).

For a binary alphabet, ordinary and wide-sense frameproof conditions coincide: whenever a coalition disagrees at a coordinate, both alphabet symbols are already available there. Zhao–Zhang therefore applies to the binary problem when its parameter hypotheses hold. The calculation above shows that these particular theorems do not settle order ten; it does not establish that later literature left the case open.

The distinction between square-code classification and cardinality thresholds also appears in Guo's [2015 Waterloo thesis](http://hdl.handle.net/10012/9260), §3.4.4, printed page 46. Results about the minimum length admitting **more codewords than coordinates** cannot automatically classify codes with exactly ten of each. This distinction was checked in Shangguan–Ma–Ge, [*New results for traitor tracing schemes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.07719) (2016), §II, and Ge–Shangguan–Wang, [*Some intriguing upper bounds for separating hash families*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01758) (2019), §6, DOI [10.1007/s11425-017-9378-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11425-017-9378-y).

Additional public manuscripts and articles were examined for a possible later resolution:

- Niu–Cao, [*Constructions and bounds for separating hash families*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03274), *Discrete Mathematics* **341** (2018), 2627–2638, DOI [10.1016/j.disc.2018.06.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2018.06.018). Its highlighted ten-element construction is `SHF(4;10,4,{2,2})`, with different length, alphabet and separation type.
- Dalai–Della Fiore–Rescigno–Vaccaro, [*Bounds and Algorithms for Frameproof Codes and Related Combinatorial Structures*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07211), ITW 2023, 544–549, DOI [10.1109/ITW55543.2023.10160230](https://doi.org/10.1109/ITW55543.2023.10160230). Its construction and asymptotic length results did not yield the square classification. Della Fiore's [thesis](https://stefano-dellafiore.unibs.it/contents/PhD_Thesis_.pdf), especially Chapter 3, was also checked.
- Fernandez–Livieratos–Martín, [*Frameproof codes, separable codes and B₂ codes: Bounds and constructions*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12095-023-00682-y), *Cryptography and Communications* **16** (2024), 481–506, DOI [10.1007/s12095-023-00682-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12095-023-00682-y). The checked results concern constructions and asymptotic rates.
- Liu–Ma–Shangguan, [*Near Optimal Probabilistic Constructions of Frameproof Codes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07711), *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* **71** (2025), 4137–4144, DOI [10.1109/TIT.2025.3558360](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2025.3558360). The principal result determines asymptotics as alphabet size grows.
- Zhong–Huang–Zhang, [*Quantitative Frameproof Codes and Hypergraphs*](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17912v1), arXiv:2511.17912 (2025). Its exact construction theorem 1.6 requires $q\ge c\ge2$, excluding the binary case $q=2,c=3$.
- Wei–Zhang–Ge, [*Separating hash families with large universe*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19440), *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A* **216** (2025), article 106075, DOI [10.1016/j.jcta.2025.106075](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2025.106075). Its main growing-alphabet theorem excludes separation types $\{1,w\}$.
- Huang–Shangguan–Zhang–Zhao, [*Focal-free uniform hypergraphs and codes*](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23611v1), *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A* **223** (2026), article 106193, DOI [10.1016/j.jcta.2026.106193](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2026.106193). Focal-free codes have a different forbidden-configuration condition; the case $r=3$ corresponds to 2-frameproof codes. The exact-code theorem 1.6 has additional alphabet, length and divisibility hypotheses. No binary order-ten classification was found in the inspected text.

The principal unresolved coverage gap is Chengyu Sun and Xin Wang, [*New upper bounds for wide-sense frameproof codes*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10623-025-01631-x), *Designs, Codes and Cryptography* **93** (2025), 3069–3082, DOI [10.1007/s10623-025-01631-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-025-01631-x), published online 18 April 2025. Only its abstract, references and first-page preview were accessible. The abstract announces improvements for wide-sense 2-frameproof codes and general $t$-frameproof codes, as well as an alphabet-size result. **Its full theorem statements and possible consequences at length ten were not checked.** Searches of the corresponding author's institutional page, arXiv, public repository records, and exact title and DOI did not locate a public complete manuscript. The publisher page remained a subscription preview in the final recheck.

Ling Jiang, Yuhao Zhao, Xiande Zhang and Yujie Gu, [*Error-Tolerant Frameproof Codes*](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11653680/), ISIT 2026, DOI [10.1109/ISIT62367.2026.11653680](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISIT62367.2026.11653680), was checked at abstract level only. The abstract describes cover-free-family relationships and probabilistic bounds, without stating the order-ten classification. Its full text was not available for this review. It was also the sole indexed forward citation to Sun–Wang found in the final citation check; that index is incomplete.

The public text of Anu Kathuria, [*Revisiting BIBDs: 3-frameproof and 2-traceable properties for large block sizes*](https://tarupublications.com/journals/jios/volume/47/issue/6/revisiting-bibds-3-frameproof-and-2-traceable-properties-for-large-block-sizes/pdf), *Journal of Information & Optimization Sciences* **47** (2026), 2293–2303, DOI [10.47974/JIOS-2213](https://doi.org/10.47974/JIOS-2213), was screened for its stated results. These concern BIBD incidence constructions. The extracted text did not state a classification of arbitrary binary ten-by-ten matrices, but incomplete formula extraction limits this check.

The review used public primary sources, author manuscripts, theses, exact-notation and phrase searches, and forward-citation searches. The final recheck included `SHF(10;10,2,{1,3})` and spacing variants, binary 3-frameproof classification, nontrivial square codes, order ten, and the historical phrase `v = 10`. No earlier resolution was located. Citation indexes, search engines and available full texts do not cover all published work, and they cannot exclude unpublished results. The supported claim is therefore that the theorem answers the case explicitly posed in 2015 and that this review found no earlier resolution within its stated coverage.

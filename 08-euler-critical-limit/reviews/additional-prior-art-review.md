## Wolfs 2024 gives related but differently scoped theorems

Thomas Wolfs, Applications of multiple orthogonal polynomials with hypergeometric moment generating functions, Advances in Applied Mathematics 158 (2024), 102709, DOI 10.1016/j.aam.2024.102709. Public preprint checked: https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08312v2 .

Section 5.2, Theorems 5.9–5.10 prove linear independence of specified hypergeometric constants. Lemmas 5.12–5.14 estimate their explicit Hermite–Padé coefficients and error with parameter vectors of given lengths p and q; Lemma 5.14 takes the diagonal multi-index (n,...,n) and n→∞. Its hypergeometric limit is exponential or 1 according to p=q−1 or p<q−1. It is not a theorem for increasing vector length or the present harmonic binomial ratio.

Section 3.3, Theorem 3.14 and Corollary 3.15 concern scaled zero distributions. These are distributional statements about polynomial roots, not the endpoint-normalized function values needed here. No direct implication of the critical Euler limit was identified in these checked results. This is a comparison of theorem hypotheses and conclusions, not a keyword-based claim of originality.

## A 2026 Bessel search lead is a different family

Manuel Mañas, Bessel-Like Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials of Mixed Type, arXiv:2608.00781v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00781v1 . The introduction, Section 2 definitions and Section 8 scope were screened. The objects are q-by-p contour matrix weights with reciprocal-Gamma moments and explicit finite polynomial/recurrence formulas. Its Wolfs citation is the 2024 hypergeometric-moment paper. The checked statements do not give the present Euler-approximant critical ratio limit. This should not be listed as a confirmed direct citation of the 2025 Euler paper based on a search-result association alone.

The critical-series theorem and concrete lambda=1 nonconvergence are formally checked. The family's finite construction and special-function ingredients are known. Additional accessible primary theorems screened here do not directly yield the joint growing-iteration limit, but the Aptekarev survey and wider growing-dimension hypergeometric literature remain unresolved coverage.
